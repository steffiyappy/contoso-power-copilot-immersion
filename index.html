About this workshop

Two short, focused immersions. Same platform, two different audiences and depths.

What you will leave with

  • Hands-on experience with Copilot Chat, Researcher, Excel, Word, PowerPoint, and Cowork
  • One persona-specific artefact you built yourself — minutes, board paper, dashboard, or microsite
  • A clear mental model of where Copilot adds value across your function, and where it does not

What this workshop is not

  • It is not a feature walkthrough — every tool is demonstrated in a Contoso Power context
  • It is not connected to live Contoso Power systems — all data is sample / anonymised
  • It is not about replacing judgment — Copilot drafts, you decide

Exercise 1 · Daily Ops Performance — Power BI-ready summaryPersona: Operations (Thermal) · PMO

Ops-Thermal PMO ⏱ 15 minutes 📎 01 ContosoPower_Thermal_Plant_Performance.xlsx

Turn raw plant performance data into a board-quality KPI summary in 5 minutes

You receive the daily plant performance dump every morning — 6 plants, 30 days, 13 columns. Today, Copilot does the analysis and produces a one-page Pro dashboard summary ready to drop into Power BI.

Step 1 · Open Excel and load the file Excel Copilot
Download 01 ContosoPower_Thermal_Plant_Performance.xlsx, open in Excel desktop, open Copilot pane.
Step 2 · Ask Copilot to summarise Excel Copilot
Summarise the Daily Performance sheet by plant. For each plant give: average availability %, average heat rate, total forced outage hours, average SLA compliance, and a RAG status against the Targets sheet. Flag any plant that breached the red threshold on any KPI. Format as a clean table I can paste into Power BI.
Step 3 · Build the trend chart Excel Copilot
Create a chart showing daily availability % across all 6 plants for the 30-day window. Highlight any day where any plant dropped below 90%. Add a target line at 95%.
Step 4 · Generate the management commentary Excel Copilot
Write a 5-bullet management commentary on the month's performance. Quantify everything. Lead with the biggest risk. End with one recommendation.
What you take away: a Power BI-ready summary table, a trend chart, and a written commentary — in 5 minutes instead of 3 hours.

Exercise 2 · Procurement vendor evaluation → Formal minutesPersona: Procurement · Corporate Secretary

Procurement CoSec ⏱ 12 minutes 📎 02 ContosoPower_Procurement_Meeting_Transcript.docx

Teams Recap → Word: turn a vendor evaluation meeting into board-quality minutes

Always include this workflow: Teams Recap auto-transcribes the meeting, then Word transforms the transcript into a professional meeting-minutes template — with decisions, rationale, and action items.

Step 1 · Open the transcript in Word Word Copilot
Download and open 02 ContosoPower_Procurement_Meeting_Transcript.docx. This simulates the Teams Recap output you would get from a real recorded meeting.
Step 2 · Draft formal minutes Word Copilot
Convert this transcript into formal Procurement Committee minutes. Use this structure: 1) Meeting details, 2) Attendees, 3) Subject, 4) Discussion summary (one paragraph per agenda item), 5) Decision, 6) Rationale (three bullets), 7) Action items table with owner and due date. Use neutral, third-person language. No quoted speech.
Step 3 · Add a risk & compliance annex Word Copilot
Add an annex at the end titled "Risk & Compliance Notes". Pull every legal, financial and delivery risk mentioned in the transcript. For each: risk, mitigation as discussed, residual concern.
Step 4 · Generate the cover email Copilot Chat
Draft a short, professional email from me (Aishah Rahman, Procurement Lead) to the Tender Committee Secretary, attaching these minutes, requesting tabling at the next Tender Committee meeting. Mention the RM 19.8M Sumitec recommendation and the two legal conditions.
What you take away: publishable minutes + risk annex + cover email — in 12 minutes instead of 2 days of drafting and review.

Exercise 3 · PMO portfolio dashboard (HTML page)Persona: PMO · Cowork build

PMO Cowork ⏱ 15 minutes 📎 03 ContosoPower_PMO_Project_Portfolio.xlsx

Delegate a multi-step task: analyse the portfolio, then build an HTML status page

This is what Cowork is for. You describe the outcome; Cowork plans, checks in, and executes. Here we ask Cowork to read the portfolio file, identify the at-risk projects, and produce a self-contained HTML one-pager you can host or email.

Step 1 · Open Copilot Chat and switch to Cowork Cowork
Upload 03 ContosoPower_PMO_Project_Portfolio.xlsx as a working file.
Step 2 · Give Cowork the full outcome Cowork
I need a weekly PMO status one-pager. Please do the following: 1. Read the Capital Projects sheet and identify all Red and Amber projects. 2. For each Red project, pull the relevant milestones from the Milestones sheet. 3. Build a single self-contained HTML file (no external dependencies) with: a header "Contoso Power · PMO Weekly Status", a RAG summary count card row, a table of Red projects with risk summary and milestone slippage, a second table of Amber projects, and a footer with the report date. 4. Use a clean professional style — teal headers (#0E7490), white background, Inter font. Check in with me once before generating the HTML.
Step 3 · Confirm Cowork's plan, then let it produce the file
Cowork will share a plan first. Approve it. Cowork generates the HTML, which you can save, preview in the browser, and reuse weekly.
Step 4 · Iterate Cowork
Add a sparkline column showing % complete progression for each Red project. Also add a "Next Action" column for each project — infer from the milestone data what the next action should be.
What you take away: a working HTML dashboard you can rerun every Friday by just dropping the latest Excel into Cowork.

Tips & promptsGet more from Copilot

  • Be specific about format. "Output as a markdown table with these columns…" beats "summarise this".
  • Reference files explicitly. Use "@filename" or "Reference a file" so Copilot grounds on the right document, not the web.
  • Iterate, don't restart. Ask for a revision rather than re-prompting from scratch.
  • For Cowork, describe the outcome, not the steps. Cowork plans the steps for you.
  • Always sanity-check numbers and named entities. Copilot drafts; you verify.
